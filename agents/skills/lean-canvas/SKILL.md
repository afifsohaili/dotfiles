# Lean Canvas

A one-page, problem-first hypothesis map for a venture that is not yet proven. Its job is **not** to describe the business — it is to make the riskiest assumption visible so you know what to test first. Treat every box as a guess until evidence says otherwise. Revise by facts, not wishes.

Two phases. Do not start Phase B until Phase A's exit criterion is met.

## Phase A — Draft (problem-first)

Fill in this order. Each box ends with a **completion criterion** — the box is not done until the criterion holds. Techniques are inline; worked examples and deeper instruments are behind the pointer at the end.

**1. Problem** — top 1–3. Frame each as **external / internal / philosophical**, or reach it with **5 Whys** if stuck. For each, record how the customer solves it *today* (existing alternatives) — often **"nothing" or a hack** (ask a colleague, slides on a call), not only competitors.
*Criterion:* every problem has ≥1 existing alternative. If you cannot name how they cope today, it is not a real problem yet.

**2. Customer Segments** — brain-dump, then pick **one** to start (two only when buyer ≠ user, e.g. marketplaces). **Early adopters** = the subset with an **above-average need** for exactly this problem.
*Criterion:* narrow enough that a named role "feels addressed"; early adopters named. Fails if it reads like "everyone."

**3. Unique Value Proposition** — the promise, in **one sentence**: desired **end result** + **timeframe** + **objection handled**, showing difference vs the alternatives in box 1. **High-level concept** sub-line: an **"X for Y"** analogy or **"we are a \_\_ that helps \_\_."**
*Criterion:* sayable in one breath; a stranger gets *why different* without a page or an hour.

**4. Solution** — copy each problem across and write the matching fix; several problems may share one solution.
*Criterion:* **1:1 traceability** — every problem has a solution and every solution maps to a problem. No orphan features, no unsolved problems.

**5. Channels** — list **inbound** (they find you: SEO, social, referral) and **outbound** (you reach first: cold email, ads, first-degree network). Choose the **one** where the early adopters hang out — the Broadway channel (Phase B). Park the rest.
*Criterion:* one primary channel chosen; the rest explicitly parked, not "all of them."

**6. Revenue Streams** — price from the **value to the customer of solving the problem**, not cost-plus. State a model (one-time / subscription / take-rate / …) and a rough LTV/CAC sense.
*Criterion:* a price and a model are stated; "what monetizable value do we provide?" is answered.

**7. Cost Structure** — fixed + variable, **scoped to a milestone** (release, first 100 customers), framed as **cost to get the first customer**.
*Criterion:* bounded to a milestone, so it can be balanced against box 6 for break-even and payback.

**8. Key Metrics** — the **smallest outcome that equals success at a horizon** (e.g. "X two years from now"), plus the **funnel math**: macro (revenue target ÷ deal size ÷ win-rate ÷ lead→opp = leads needed) and micro benchmarks (e.g. 20% landing, 10% lead→opp, 20% win).
*Criterion:* one headline metric and the macro funnel that produces it are written down.

**9. Unfair Advantage** — what cannot be **bought or copied** (reputation, exclusive data, authority, community). **Blank is acceptable** early — leave it as an open risk, do not fabricate one.
*Criterion:* either a genuinely uncopyable edge, or explicitly marked "TBD — open risk."

**Phase A exit:** all nine criteria hold **and** you have named the single **riskiest assumption** — the box most likely to be wrong and most fatal if it is. Only then proceed.

## Phase B — Test, then scale

1. **Rank the boxes by uncertainty × blast radius.** The riskiest assumption is almost always in Problem (is it real and felt?) or Customer Segments (do these specific people have it acutely?). Start there.
2. **Run a Broadway show** — the smallest test of that assumption, in the **one channel** from box 5, as **V1**. Validation menu (interviews, day-in-the-life, concierge, landing page, small prototype) is in references.
3. **Read the math** — compare observed micro conversions to the benchmarks in box 8, and check the macro funnel still closes to the revenue target.
4. **Decide:** **revise** (loop back to the failing box and re-run Phase A's criterion) or **scale** (master the working channel first, then add channels one at a time). Do not scale an unvalidated thesis.

## Anti-patterns

- **Everyone is a customer** — segment too broad to resonate with anyone.
- **Existing alternatives = only competitors** — forgetting "doing nothing" and hacks, the most common incumbents.
- **Orphan solutions** — a feature with no mapped problem, or a problem with no solution (breaks box 4).
- **UVP needs a page** — if it takes an hour to explain, the promise is not nailed (box 3).
- **Premature scaling** — turning on channels or hiring agencies before the thesis is validated; the classic way to burn 12–18 months (Phase B exists to prevent this).
- **Cost-plus pricing** — pricing from your costs instead of the customer's value (box 6).
- **Canvas as scripture** — treating guesses as facts; never revising after evidence.

## Output

Produce: the filled nine-box canvas, the named **riskiest assumption**, and the proposed **first test** (one channel, V1, what evidence would confirm or kill it).
