# Lean Canvas — references

Disclosed reference for `SKILL.md`. Reach for these only when a branch needs them (worked example, deeper instrument, or a limits check).

## Worked example A — Uber (two-sided; passenger vs driver)

- **Problem** — Passengers: expensive taxis, cab availability, long rush-hour waits, advance booking often required, no driver profile. Drivers: need extra income, part-time opportunity, want to know who they drive.
- **Existing alternatives** — Passengers: public transport, classic taxi, ride with a friend. Drivers: taxi service, other full/part-time jobs.
- **Customer Segments** — Passengers; Drivers (full-time vs supplemental). **Early adopters:** people already riding public transport / classic taxis (active need).
- **UVP** — Passengers: all services in one app, a safe ride 24/7/365. Drivers: your own schedule, manage revenue, in-app navigation, no taxi-license hassle. **High-level concept:** "Uber is like a taxi, but cheaper, safer, and more flexible."
- **Solution** — cheaper ride, wide car network, fast pickup, route tracking, driver/passenger ratings.
- **Channels** — word of mouth, PR, user referrals.
- **Revenue** — ~25% take per ride (value-based).
- **Cost** — product development, infrastructure & support, marketing, salaries.
- **Key metrics** — # users, # rides, money earned.
- **Unfair advantage** — early: the experience (low-priced rides at a button-push); later: network effects.

## Worked example B — online-collaboration bootcamp (Scrum masters)

- **Problem** — External: no experience with online-collab tools, back-to-back meetings, unsure which tools. Internal: unsure they can match in-office results, frustrated/behind, confused. Philosophical: worked hard to be seen as experts; forced online, they deserve equal results.
- **Existing alternatives** — ask a more-experienced colleague; present current slides over a video call (hacks/status quo, not competitors).
- **Customer Segments** — facilitators/Scrum masters new to online collaboration. **Early adopters:** those forced online with back-to-back meetings, doubting they can match office results.
- **UVP** — "Online teamwork on your own terms — learn async collaboration" (work together online, productively, on your terms, within 5 weeks, without feeling behind). **High-level concept:** "a gym bootcamp, but for working online."
- **Solution** — learn from real-life examples + a safe environment to practice the tools (one solution serving two problems).
- **Channels** — Inbound: weekly YouTube, LinkedIn posts, SEO, templates/referral. Outbound: YouTube/LinkedIn ads, LinkedIn DMs. **Primary (usable now):** weekly YouTube + LinkedIn DMs + SEO.
- **Revenue** — one-time fee $2,500 (alt: subscription).
- **Key metric** — smallest success outcome at a 2-year horizon ("X two years from now").
- **Cost** — fixed (hosting, software) + variable (outside help); cost-to-first-customer.
- **Unfair advantage** — community + YouTube content library.
- **Riskiest assumption → first test:** sure the segment was Scrum masters, unsure the problems were truly felt → **empathize first** (interviews / day-in-the-life / small prototype) before scaling.

## Origin — Business Model Canvas → Lean Canvas

Maurya adapted Osterwalder's canvas (built for established businesses) for startups by swapping four boxes: **Key Partners → Problem**, **Key Activities → Solution**, **Key Resources → Key Metrics**, **Customer Relationships → Unfair Advantage**. The shift is the point: from describing a running business to a problem–solution, customer-focused hypothesis map.

## Deeper instruments behind the boxes

The canvas is the one-page distillation; these sit behind specific boxes when more rigor is warranted.

- **ICP rigor** (behind box 2) — a real Ideal Customer Profile is a written definition of the segment for *this* stage of growth, distinct from TAM; it forces tough choices (you cannot go both ways). Multi-point ICP checklists exist (e.g. TK Kader's) — use one when "narrow" still feels vague.
- **Strategic narrative / manifesto** (behind box 3) — the longer story behind the one-sentence UVP: the change you bring, why you are different, why act now. It feeds the lead magnet, website hero, pitch deck, and sales deck.
- **Macro + micro funnel math** (behind box 8) — Macro example: $1M target ÷ $100k deal = 10 customers ÷ 20% win-rate = 50 opportunities ÷ 10% lead→opp = **500 leads needed**. Micro benchmarks to compare against: ~20% landing-page conversion, ~10% lead→opportunity, ~20% win-rate.
- **Validation menu** (Phase B) — user interviews, day-in-the-life/shadowing, concierge MVP, landing-page smoke test, or a small prototype to learn from real interactions. Pick the smallest that can confirm or kill the riskiest assumption.

## Limits / when not to use

- **Redundancy risk** — Problem↔Segment and Solution↔UVP can blur. Mitigated by the box-1 and box-4 criteria (alternatives prove the problem; 1:1 traceability keeps the solution honest).
- **Speed over completeness** — a hypothesis map, not a strategy document: no vision, no explicit trade-offs, no coherence check across choices. Pair with the manifesto and funnel math for coherence; consider **Startup Canvas** (Paweł Huryn) when you need strategy and business model together.
- **Not for established businesses** — use Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas instead.
- **Single-moat trap** — "unfair advantage" names one edge, but real defensibility is often the integrated whole. Do not over-index on a single moat.
